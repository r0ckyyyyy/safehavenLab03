What Is Domestic & Gender-Based Violence?

Domestic violence is any behaviour used to control or hurt a spouse, ex-partner, child or family member. Gender-based violence is harm done to someone because of their gender, at home, at work, in public or online.

Physical

Hitting, slapping, choking, pushing or using a weapon.

Emotional

Insults, threats, isolation, constant monitoring and humiliation.

Economic

Controlling money, stopping you from working, taking your income.

Sexual

Forced sex, unwanted touching or sexual harassment.

Digital

Tracking your phone, stalking, sharing private images.

Warning signs

  • Fear of your partner's reaction
  • Cut off from family and friends
  • Threats if you try to leave

Law Enforcement & Legal Protection

Domestic violence is a crime in Malaysia, not a private family matter. These are the main laws that protect you.

LawWhat it does
Domestic Violence Act 1994 (Act 521), amended 2017Protects spouses, ex-spouses, children and family members. Covers emotional abuse and provides protection orders.
Penal Code (Act 574)Criminal offences such as causing hurt, rape, intimidation, outrage of modesty and stalking.
Child Act 2001 (Act 611)Protects children from abuse and neglect.
Sexual Offences Against Children Act 2017Child sexual crimes, grooming and child pornography.
Anti-Sexual Harassment Act 2022 (Act 840)Sets up a Tribunal to hear sexual harassment complaints.
Islamic Family Law (State)For Muslims: divorce and maintenance through the Syariah Court.

Protection orders under Act 521

Interim Protection Order

Issued by the court to protect you while a complaint is investigated.

Emergency Protection Order

Quick, short-term protection issued by a Social Welfare Officer.

Protection Order

Issued by the Magistrate's Court to stop the abuser from harming you.

General information only, not legal advice. Confirm details with a lawyer, Legal Aid Department or an NGO.

Enforcer: Who Can Help You?

These agencies and organisations work together to protect victims and act against offenders.

Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM)

Takes reports, investigates and arrests offenders. Specialist unit: D11 Sexual, Women & Child Investigation.

Social Welfare Dept. (JKM)

Issues Emergency Protection Orders and provides shelter, counselling and aid.

Hospitals (OSCC)

One Stop Crisis Centres in government hospitals give free treatment and counselling.

Courts & Legal Aid

Courts issue protection orders. Legal Aid Department helps those who qualify.

NGOs

WAO, WCC and AWAM offer shelter, counselling and legal information.

Ministry (KPWKM)

Leads national policy for women, family and child protection; runs Talian Kasih.

What to do, step by step

  1. Get to a safe place such as a trusted person's home or a shelter.
  2. Call for help: 999 in an emergency or Talian Kasih 15999.
  3. See a doctor and ask for your injuries to be recorded.
  4. Make a police report and ask for a copy.
  5. Keep evidence: photos, medical reports, messages, dates.
  6. Apply for a protection order with JKM, an NGO or a lawyer.
